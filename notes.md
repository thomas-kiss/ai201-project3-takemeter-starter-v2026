## Milestone 1. Community and reading

**Community:** r/nba. Public, no login wall. Not centered on one
show/release. New game threads every night so no spoiler problem.

**What I read:** About 40 real comments from a recent post-game discussion
thread (76ers vs. Knicks, ring night). Pulled via Reddit's public comments
RSS feed. Comments, not top-level posts. Top-level r/nba posts are mostly
one-line link or highlight titles. No body text. Not enough signal for a
text classifier. The comments under game threads are where people actually
argue.

**Candidate distinctions noticed:**

1. Some comments cite a specific, checkable detail. A stat line, a quote, a
   named play ("39 points collected... of a possible 45", a TV-replay
   breakdown of a defensive sequence). Most don't.
2. Some are confident one-line opinions with no support offered ("highest
   ceiling of any contender", "cheat code").
3. A large share, maybe 25-30%, is pure banter or jokes or reactions with no
   argument at all. Fantasy football gripes, rival-team jokes, emoji
   reactions.
4. Some are short reactions tied to something that just happened, without
   building toward an argument ("Wore a jersey tonight, but X is my favorite
   player now").
