## Milestone 1 — Community and reading

**Community:** r/nba (public, no login wall, not centered on a single
show/release — new game threads every night so no spoiler problem).

**What I read:** ~40 real comments pulled from a recent post-game discussion
thread (76ers vs. Knicks, ring night) via Reddit's public comments RSS feed.
Comments, not top-level posts, because top-level r/nba posts are mostly
one-line link/highlight titles with no body text — not enough signal for a
text classifier. The comments under game threads are where people actually
argue.

**Candidate distinctions noticed:**

1. Some comments cite a specific, checkable detail — a stat line, a quote, a
   named play ("39 points collected... of a possible 45", a TV-replay
   breakdown of a specific defensive sequence). Most don't.
2. Some are confident one-line evaluative claims with no support offered
   ("highest ceiling of any contender", "cheat code").
3. A large share (~25-30%) is pure banter/jokes/reactions with no argument at
   all (fantasy football gripes, rival-team jokes, emoji reactions).
4. Some are short factual-observational reactions tied to something that just
   happened, without building toward an argument ("Wore a jersey tonight,
   but X is my favorite player now").
